Answer
No. There is no cannibalization. The event's revenue is additional.

The event only sells Friday to Monday. Tuesday to Thursday it earns nothing, so those days act as a built-in control. Regular-store revenue per player fell by an identical amount on both — 27.4% on event days, 29.1% on control days.

Its buyers are overwhelmingly existing customers (64.8%), but they did not cut back. Matched against a control group of non-buyers, their regular-store spend moved identically, and 42% of them buy a regular pack in the same weekend they buy the event. They added it to the basket rather than swapping into it.

The test that answers the question

Core evidence · iOS

Blitz Sale sells Friday to Monday only. On Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday it earns essentially nothing — $17 across seven weeks. Those three days are therefore a clean control group sitting inside our own data.

The test: take regular-store revenue per active player and compare how far it fell on event days against control days, before launch versus after. Cannibalization would show up as a bigger fall on event days.

Did it hurt existing customers?

Buyer-level · iOS

Yes, its buyers are existing customers — 71.0% had already paid for something before, against roughly 25% across the payer base as a whole. That is precisely why cannibalization was the right question. If an offer sells almost entirely to people who already spend, the obvious risk is that it just redirects money they were going to spend anyway.

Four tests, each looking at those buyers directly rather than at aggregate revenue.

The one thing that did move. Blitz Sale buyers made 47.4% fewer regular-store transactions after their first purchase, against 43.0% for the control — a 4-point gap. But their spend fell identically. Fewer, larger purchases, not less money. Worth a second look if it widens, but it does not change the revenue conclusion.

Why ARPPU looks worse on event days — and why it is not a problem. ARPPU is revenue divided by payers. On event days the event pulled in extra payers who spend small amounts, so the divisor grew and the average per payer fell. Multiply conversion by ARPPU and you get revenue per active player, which is the number that actually matters — and on that measure event days beat control days by 2.9 percentage points. Conversion up, ARPPU down, revenue up. That is a wider payer base, not a weaker one.

What is actually falling

Separate issue

The 27% drop that triggered this investigation is real, but it is business-wide. It is present on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, where Blitz Sale has never sold a single unit. It started before the event scaled and has continued through it.

This points at acquisition quality, pricing, or the regular catalogue — not at the weekend offer. It needs a separate investigation, and removing Blitz Sale would not touch it.
